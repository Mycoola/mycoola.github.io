This part covers general LLM/RAG fundamentals that transfer across any AI platform, plus the Einstein Trust Layer's specific approach to enterprise AI governance and data protection.

LLM Fundamentals

1.What is a foundation model, and how does it differ from a fine-tuned model?

A foundation model is a large, general-purpose model pretrained on broad data, capable of many tasks out of the box via prompting. A fine-tuned model has been further trained on a narrower, task-specific dataset to specialize its behavior — generally more effort/cost but potentially better performance on that narrow task than prompting alone can achieve.

2.What is the context window of an LLM, and why does it constrain solution design?

The context window is the maximum amount of text (measured in tokens) a model can process in a single call, including both the prompt/instructions and any grounding data. It constrains design because you can't simply dump unlimited data into a prompt — you must select or retrieve only the most relevant context to fit within the limit.

3.What is a token, and why do LLM providers measure usage in tokens rather than words or characters?

A token is a sub-word unit of text the model actually processes (roughly ¾ of a word on average in English). Providers measure in tokens because that's the model's true unit of computation and cost, and it varies by language/tokenizer — not a simple word count.

4.What are the main risks of relying on an LLM for a business-critical decision without human review?

Hallucination (confidently stated but incorrect information), inconsistency across similar inputs, and susceptibility to manipulated input — for decisions with real financial, legal, or safety consequences, a human review or approval step is generally still warranted regardless of how capable the model appears in testing.

5.What does it mean for a model to "hallucinate," and why does it happen?

Hallucination is when a model generates plausible-sounding but factually incorrect or fabricated content, stated with the same confidence as accurate content. It happens because the model is fundamentally predicting likely next tokens based on patterns, not verifying facts against a ground truth — which is exactly why grounding with real data matters so much.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

6.What is Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and why is it used instead of fine-tuning?

RAG retrieves relevant information from an external knowledge source at query time and injects it into the prompt as context, rather than baking knowledge into the model's weights via fine-tuning. It's preferred when the underlying knowledge changes frequently (fine-tuning would require expensive retraining) or when you need the model to cite/ground its answer in specific, verifiable source data.

7.What is a vector embedding, and how does semantic search differ from keyword search?

A vector embedding is a numerical representation of text that captures its semantic meaning, allowing similarity to be measured mathematically (via distance between vectors). Semantic search finds results based on meaning similarity even without exact keyword overlap, while keyword search matches literal terms — semantic search finds "car troubles" when you search "vehicle problems," which keyword search alone would miss.

8.What is a vector database, and what role does it play in a RAG pipeline?

A vector database stores embeddings and supports fast nearest-neighbor similarity search across potentially millions of vectors. In a RAG pipeline, it's the retrieval component — given a query's embedding, it quickly finds the most semantically similar stored documents/chunks to inject into the prompt as context.

9.What is "chunking" in a RAG pipeline, and why does chunk size matter?

Chunking splits large source documents into smaller segments before embedding/indexing them, since retrieving an entire document when only one paragraph is relevant wastes context window space. Chunk size is a trade-off — too small loses surrounding context, too large dilutes retrieval precision and wastes tokens.

10.How do you evaluate whether a RAG system is retrieving the right information?

Test with a representative set of realistic queries and check whether the retrieved chunks actually contain the information needed to answer correctly (retrieval quality), separately from whether the final generated answer was correct (generation quality) — conflating the two makes it harder to diagnose which part of the pipeline is failing.

11.What is the difference between RAG and simply pasting all relevant documents into the prompt?

RAG selectively retrieves only the most relevant subset of a potentially huge knowledge base, which is necessary once your knowledge base exceeds what fits in a context window (or would be prohibitively expensive/slow to include in full every time) — pasting everything only works for small, static knowledge bases.

12.How does Salesforce's native grounding (e.g., in Prompt Builder) relate to the general concept of RAG?

It's a purpose-built implementation of the same underlying idea — pulling relevant, current data (records, related objects, Data Cloud data) into a prompt at generation time — but abstracted into declarative tooling rather than requiring you to build the retrieval/embedding infrastructure yourself.

Einstein Trust Layer

13.What is the Einstein Trust Layer, and what specific risks does it mitigate?

It's Salesforce's trust and governance framework sitting between the platform and any underlying LLM, mitigating risks like sensitive data leaking to a third-party model, ungoverned/unaudited AI usage, and inconsistent data handling across different AI features — providing a consistent security and compliance layer regardless of which model is used underneath.

14.How does data masking work before a prompt reaches an external LLM?

Sensitive data patterns (e.g., identified as PII) can be automatically detected and masked or replaced with placeholders before the prompt is sent to the model, then the actual values can be restored in the response — reducing the exposure of sensitive data to the underlying model provider.

15.What audit/logging capability exists for AI-generated content and actions in Salesforce?

Prompts, responses, and agent actions can be logged for audit purposes, giving admins visibility into what was generated, what data was used to ground it, and what actions were taken — critical for troubleshooting issues and demonstrating compliance in regulated industries.

16.Why does Salesforce support multiple underlying LLM providers rather than a single proprietary model?

It gives customers flexibility to choose a model that fits their cost, performance, or compliance requirements (some industries/regions have restrictions on specific providers), while the Trust Layer provides a consistent governance experience regardless of which model is actually selected underneath.

17.What does "zero data retention" mean in the context of an LLM provider agreement, and why does it matter for enterprise customers?

It means the LLM provider commits to not storing or using submitted prompts/data to train future models. It matters because enterprise customers need assurance their proprietary or customer data won't leak into a model's future training data or be retained beyond the immediate request.

Governance & Compliance

18.How do you handle a regulated industry's compliance requirements when enabling generative AI features?

Involve compliance/legal early to identify specific constraints (data residency, specific prohibited use cases, required disclosures to end users), configure the Trust Layer's data masking and model choice accordingly, and maintain audit logs sufficient to demonstrate compliance during a review.

19.What is a common compliance concern specific to using AI in customer-facing financial or healthcare contexts?

Ensuring the AI doesn't provide advice that legally requires a licensed professional (financial advice, medical diagnosis) without appropriate disclaimers and human oversight, and that sensitive regulated data (health records, financial account details) is handled according to the specific regulatory framework (HIPAA, financial services regulations) governing that data.

20.How would you design AI feature rollout to satisfy an internal security review before go-live?

Document what data is used for grounding, what model(s) process it, what masking/retention policies apply, what guardrails prevent misuse, and what audit trail exists — treating it as a formal review artifact rather than an informal conversation, similar to any other new system touching sensitive data.

Ethical & Responsible AI Considerations

21.What is AI bias, and how can it manifest in a business application?

Bias occurs when a model's outputs systematically favor or disadvantage certain groups, often because of patterns in its training data. In a business application this might show up as inconsistent service quality or recommendations across different customer segments — worth actively testing for, not just assuming won't happen.

22.Why is transparency about AI involvement important in a customer-facing feature?

Customers generally have a right to know when they're interacting with an AI system rather than a human, both as a matter of trust and, in some jurisdictions, as an emerging legal/regulatory requirement — designing this transparency in from the start avoids a costly retrofit later.

23.How do you balance AI feature velocity (shipping fast) against responsible AI review processes?

Build lightweight, proportionate review gates scaled to actual risk (a low-risk internal tool needs less scrutiny than a customer-facing financial feature) rather than either skipping review entirely or applying the same heavy process to everything regardless of risk level.

24.What responsibility does a Salesforce architect have when recommending an AI-driven feature to a client?

Being honest about the technology's current limitations (accuracy isn't perfect, some hallucination risk always exists) rather than overselling it, and ensuring the client understands what human oversight/guardrails are still necessary — architects who oversell AI capability set clients up for a trust-damaging failure later.

25.How do you keep up with the fast pace of change in AI platform capabilities as an architect or developer?

Regularly review release notes for new AI features/model updates, test new capabilities in a sandbox before recommending them to clients, and maintain healthy skepticism toward marketing claims until you've verified behavior yourself against your own realistic test scenarios.
