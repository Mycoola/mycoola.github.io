Architecture

Architecture Principles Q&A

August 2026  ·  Data, security, delivery, and product architecture

A practical set of notes from reading about agile data governance, Salesforce sharing and security, record-triggered automation, continuous delivery, DevOps, AI platforms, and Scrum. The common thread is architectural feedback: make important rules visible, testable, and close to the work that depends on them.

Data and Platform Architecture

1.What makes data governance agile rather than bureaucratic?

Agile governance gives teams useful guardrails while keeping decisions close to the people building and using the data. It favors shared standards, embedded data expertise, automation, and feedback from real usage over a distant approval committee that reviews every change.

2.Why should governance be treated as part of delivery?

Data quality, privacy, retention, and access are delivery concerns because they affect how a feature behaves in production. When those rules are explicit and automated in the delivery workflow, teams can detect violations early instead of discovering them after adoption.

3.What is the purpose of a Salesforce sharing architecture?

Sharing architecture defines who can access which records and why. It combines organization-wide defaults, role hierarchy, sharing rules, teams, manual sharing, and programmatic mechanisms so access follows business responsibility without making every object broadly visible.

4.Why should record access and field access be designed separately?

A user may need to find a record without being allowed to see every field on it. Record-level sharing answers which records are visible; object and field permissions answer which data can be read or changed. Separating the layers makes least-privilege decisions easier to reason about.

5.What does a defense-in-depth security model add?

Defense in depth assumes that one control can be misconfigured or bypassed. Identity, permissions, record sharing, field security, secure coding, monitoring, and operational review each reduce a different part of the risk, so a failure in one layer does not expose everything.

Automation and Delivery

6.What should be checked before choosing a record-triggered flow design?

Start with timing and transaction boundaries: whether the change must happen before save, after save, or asynchronously. Then check entry criteria, recursion, bulk behavior, related-record updates, fault handling, and whether the automation remains understandable when the object gains more rules.

7.Why are entry conditions important for record-triggered automation?

Entry conditions prevent an automation from running for records and changes that do not matter. That reduces unnecessary work, lowers the chance of recursion, and makes the business event that starts the flow explicit to future maintainers.

8.What is continuous delivery?

Continuous delivery is the capability to keep software in a releasable state through an automated, repeatable path from change to validation to deployment. It does not require releasing every commit; it requires making release a low-risk, well-practiced operation.

9.How is continuous delivery different from continuous deployment?

Continuous delivery prepares and validates changes so a release can happen on demand, often with a human approval step. Continuous deployment goes further by automatically releasing changes that pass the required checks. Both depend on reliable tests and deployment automation.

10.What architectural habits make DevOps effective?

DevOps works when teams share ownership of delivery and operations, shorten feedback loops, automate repeatable work, measure outcomes, and design for observability. Tools help, but the central change is treating build, release, reliability, and learning as one system.

Product and Organizational Architecture

11.What should an AI-agent platform provide beyond a language model?

An agent platform needs reliable access to business context, tools, permissions, action boundaries, observability, and evaluation. The model supplies reasoning and language generation, but the platform determines what the agent can know, do, and safely explain.

12.Why do agent architectures need explicit trust boundaries?

An agent may combine user instructions, retrieved data, tool results, and generated plans. Explicit trust boundaries define which inputs are authoritative, which actions require confirmation, and how sensitive data is handled before an agent can affect a system of record.

13.Why does an organization's structure matter to architecture?

Architecture follows communication paths and decision rights. Team boundaries, ownership, and reporting relationships influence how quickly information travels and how easily a design can evolve, so an architecture that ignores organization often creates coordination bottlenecks.

14.How do Scrum goals improve technical decision-making?

A clear product goal gives the team a durable direction, while a sprint goal creates a short-term focus. Together they help the team evaluate tradeoffs by asking whether a technical choice advances the outcome, rather than merely increasing activity or scope.

15.What principle connects governance, security, delivery, and Scrum?

Make intent visible and feedback fast. A good architecture states who owns a decision, what constraints apply, how a change will be tested, and what signal shows success. This keeps control from becoming paperwork and keeps flexibility from becoming guesswork.
