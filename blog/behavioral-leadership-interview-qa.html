Behavioral & Leadership Interview Q&A

Michael George  ·  Updated August 2026  ·  8 min read

The technical questions get you shortlisted; the behavioral round decides the offer. These are the STAR-format answers, elevator pitch, and closing questions I use across Salesforce Architect, Technical Manager, and Principal Developer interviews — kept current as new patterns come up.

Elevator Pitch

Q: Tell me about yourself.

"I'm Michael George, a 13-times Salesforce-certified professional with over 15 years in software development, including 7+ years deeply focused on Salesforce. I've led enterprise CRM implementations across automotive, real estate, healthcare, logistics, and non-profit industries. Most recently at Capgemini, I served as Salesforce Technical Manager and Lead Solutions Consultant, managing multi-country deployments like Stellantis across France, Germany, UK, Italy, and Spain. I hold the Application Architect and Data Architect certifications, and I specialize in designing scalable architectures and leading complex integrations. I'm now looking for a senior architect or principal developer role where I can drive digital transformation at scale."

Leadership & Team Management

Q: Tell me about a time you led a team through a complex project.

Situation: At Capgemini, I was appointed Technical Manager for the Stellantis Salesforce CRM — a multi-country, multi-language enterprise implementation across France, Germany, UK, Italy, and Spain.
Task: Lead a team of developers and consultants to maintain operational excellence and deliver continuous platform improvements.
Action: I established regular cadences with regional stakeholders, ran code reviews, prioritized the backlog by business impact, and implemented CI/CD deployment practices using Salesforce DX.
Result: High service standards sustained over 2+ years, with improved first-call resolution rates and zero critical deployment failures.

Q: Describe a time you mentored a junior developer.

Situation: At CloudzLab, junior developers were struggling with Salesforce governor limits and async Apex patterns.
Task: Bring them up to speed without slowing delivery timelines.
Action: I created internal code review checklists and held weekly knowledge-sharing sessions on Apex best practices, SOQL optimization, and integration patterns.
Result: Measurable improvement in code quality — fewer bugs in QA and faster PR approvals.

Problem Solving

Q: Tell me about the most complex Salesforce bug you ever debugged.

Situation: At IQVIA, an integration with a third-party pharma data provider was intermittently failing, with no governor limit hits and no exceptions in the logs.
Task: Find the root cause without a clear error trail.
Action: Traced it to a race condition in a Queueable chain — bulk-inserted records were spawning competing jobs for the same external ID.
Result: Fixed with a Batch Apex re-architecture, and added a custom Integration_Log__c object for full visibility going forward.

Questions to Ask the Interviewer

  1. "What does the Salesforce architecture look like today — how many orgs, clouds, and integrations are in scope?"
  2. "What's the biggest technical challenge the team is facing right now?"
  3. "How does the team currently handle deployments — is there a CI/CD pipeline?"
  4. "What does success look like for this role in the first 90 days?"
  5. "How involved is this role in pre-sales / client-facing architecture discussions?"
  6. "What certifications or skills would you most like this person to develop further?"

Salary Negotiation

Q: What are your salary expectations?

"Based on my 15 years of experience and 13 Salesforce certifications including Application Architect and Data Architect, combined with my track record leading enterprise multi-country implementations, I'm targeting [X]. However, I'm open to discussing the full compensation package including benefits, bonuses, and growth opportunities."

Red flags I now listen for during the same conversation: no clear org consolidation strategy, no version control or metadata deployment process, being expected to be both architect and full-time admin, or a rush straight to implementation without discovery.
