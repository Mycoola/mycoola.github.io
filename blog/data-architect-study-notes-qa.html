Data Architect Study Notes Q&A

August 2026  ·  Original study notes, not reproduced exam questions

Original, generically-written study notes on the concept areas covered by the Salesforce Certified Data Architect credential. These are not reproductions of specific exam questions; they cover the underlying knowledge needed to design and evaluate data models at scale.

1.What should drive the decision between master-detail and lookup relationships in a data model?

Master-detail enforces ownership, cascading delete, and roll-up summaries but ties the child record's security and sharing to the parent. Lookup gives more independence between objects. The choice depends on whether the child record's lifecycle and access should be strictly bound to its parent.

2.Why does large data volume (LDV) planning need to happen before implementation rather than after?

Index selection, sharing architecture, and object relationships all affect query and load performance at scale. Retrofitting these decisions after millions of records already exist is far more disruptive than designing for expected volume from the start.

3.What is the tradeoff between a single large custom object and splitting data across multiple related objects?

A single large object can simplify queries but risks hitting row-level and field-count limits and can create maintenance complexity as unrelated concerns get bundled together. Splitting objects clarifies data ownership but requires more relationships and joins to reassemble a full picture.

4.How should an architect approach a data migration strategy for moving a legacy system into Salesforce?

Key considerations include data cleansing before migration (garbage in, garbage out), mapping legacy IDs to new Salesforce IDs for traceability, staged/batched loads to respect governor limits, and a validation plan comparing record counts and key field values post-migration.

5.What role does master data management (MDM) play when Salesforce is one of several systems holding customer data?

MDM establishes a single source of truth and synchronization rules across systems, preventing Salesforce and other systems from silently drifting into inconsistent views of the same customer over time — a concern that grows as more systems integrate with the same underlying data.

6.Why does field-level data governance (naming conventions, required fields, picklist management) matter architecturally, not just administratively?

Inconsistent field usage undermines reporting accuracy and downstream integrations that assume consistent data shapes. Architecture decisions about validation rules, required fields, and picklist restrictions directly affect whether the data model can be trusted at scale.

7.What should an architect consider when recommending Big Objects for a use case?

Big Objects support massive-scale storage (billions of records) with reduced indexing and query flexibility compared to standard/custom objects — appropriate for archival or audit-trail data that needs to persist at scale but doesn't require complex ad hoc querying.

These notes are original summaries of publicly documented Salesforce data architecture concepts (Salesforce Architects and Designing Record Access for Enterprise Scale documentation), written to support certification study without reproducing any proprietary exam content.
