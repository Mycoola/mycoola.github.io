Sharing & Visibility Architect/Designer Study Notes Q&A

August 2026  ·  Original study notes, not reproduced exam questions

These are original, generically-written study notes covering the core concept areas tested by the Salesforce Sharing and Visibility Architect/Designer credentials. They are not reproductions of any specific exam question — the certification exams themselves are proprietary content, and copying their questions here would raise copyright concerns. Instead, this covers the underlying concepts a candidate needs to understand deeply enough to answer novel scenario questions on the real exam.

Sharing Model Fundamentals

1.What is the correct order of evaluation when determining a user's access to a record?

Record access is the most permissive result across all applicable mechanisms: organization-wide defaults set the baseline, then role hierarchy, sharing rules, manual sharing, team access, and Apex-managed sharing can each grant additional access on top — none of these can revoke access once another mechanism has granted it.

2.When is Apex Managed Sharing required instead of a declarative sharing rule?

Apex Managed Sharing is needed when the sharing logic depends on conditions declarative sharing rules can't express — for example, a private sharing model where access must be granted based on complex, dynamic criteria (like specific field value combinations) that criteria-based sharing rules cannot evaluate.

3.What is the tradeoff of setting an object's OWD to Private versus a more open default?

Private OWDs give maximum control (nothing is visible unless explicitly granted) but require more deliberate sharing configuration to give legitimate users access; a more open default reduces configuration overhead at the cost of exposing more data by default.

Teams and Territory-Based Access

4.What is the purpose of Account Teams and Opportunity Teams, and how do they differ from role-based sharing?

Teams let specific users be added to a specific record (not a whole role) with a defined access level, useful when collaboration on a record doesn't map cleanly to the org's role hierarchy — for example, a specialist who needs access to one deal but isn't in the owning rep's management chain.

5.What is the difference between original and Enterprise Territory Management in terms of sharing implications?

Enterprise Territory Management supports assigning a record to multiple territories with distinct access rules per territory model, giving finer-grained control than original Territory Management's simpler single-hierarchy structure — a meaningful difference for orgs restructuring sales territories.

6.Why would large-scale role hierarchy realignment require special tools rather than a simple bulk update?

Changing roles for many users at once triggers a large sharing recalculation across every record those users own or have access to. Deferred sharing calculation and other bulk-realignment tools exist specifically to batch this recalculation efficiently instead of overwhelming the system with immediate, per-change recalculation.

External Users and Communities

7.Why does sharing configuration for external (community/portal) users require extra care compared to internal users?

External users often have more restrictive default visibility (like "View" access only to their own account's records), and enabling broader sharing to external users can unintentionally expose internal data across account boundaries if roles and sharing rules aren't scoped carefully.

8.What is "implicit sharing," and why does it matter for architecture decisions?

Implicit sharing grants automatic access based on relationships — for example, a contact's owner implicitly having access to related cases — without a separate manual sharing rule. Architects need to account for these implicit grants, since they can expose data in ways that aren't visible in the explicit sharing rule configuration.

Performance and Data Skew

9.Why is ownership skew (thousands of records owned by one user) treated as an architectural risk?

Every role hierarchy or ownership change involving that user triggers a massive sharing recalculation across all their owned records, which can create severe performance issues and lock contention — a known threshold (commonly cited around 10,000 records per owner) is used as a design guideline to avoid this.

10.What options exist to mitigate risks during a large-volume bulk data import regarding sharing?

Deferring sharing calculations during the load and running a single recalculation afterward avoids the system recalculating sharing after every individual record insert, which would be dramatically slower and could hit governor limits at scale.
