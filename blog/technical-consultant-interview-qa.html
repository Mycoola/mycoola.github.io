Senior Consultant / Tech Interview Q&A

Michael George  ·  Updated August 2026  ·  7 min read

Senior Consultant and "Tech Interview" rounds tend to focus less on syntax and more on how you frame trade-offs to a client. These are the solution-architecture and case-study questions I actually get asked, kept current as I go through more of these interviews.

Solution Architecture Approach

Q: Walk me through how you approach designing a Salesforce solution architecture.

  1. Discovery: requirements workshops mapping business processes to Salesforce capabilities.
  2. Data model design: object relationships, sharing model, and data architecture up front — especially critical for complex orgs (Experience Cloud, multi-business-unit).
  3. Integration strategy: identify systems of record, choose sync vs. async, design error handling and retry logic.
  4. Security & sharing: profiles, permission sets, sharing rules, and OWD designed early — never bolted on later.
  5. Scalability: governor-limit awareness, bulkified Apex, async processing with Queueable/Batch.
  6. Deployment: source-controlled with Salesforce DX, scratch orgs, CI/CD.
  7. Documentation & handoff: technical specs, data flow diagrams, runbook.

Q: How do you decide between Flow, Apex, and Process Builder?

Flow is my first choice for declarative automation — it covers roughly 80% of use cases: record-triggered flows, screen flows, scheduled flows. Apex comes in when logic is too complex for Flow: multi-object calculations, callouts, custom error handling. Process Builder is legacy — I migrate every PB I find to Flow, and never build a new one. Rule of thumb: declarative first, Apex second, and always document the "why" behind the choice.

Q: Describe your approach to Salesforce integration architecture.

I start by classifying the need: real-time, near-real-time, or batch. For real-time, REST API with Connected Apps and OAuth 2.0. For async, Platform Events or Change Data Capture. For legacy systems, middleware (MuleSoft, Dell Boomi, or custom). I always design idempotent endpoints, implement error queues, and log every integration transaction in a custom object for auditability — clients need to be able to trace a failure without opening a support ticket with me.

Client Case Studies

Q: A client wants to migrate 10 million records from their legacy CRM to Salesforce. How do you approach it?

  1. Data assessment: profile data quality, duplicates, missing required fields.
  2. Field mapping document: source → Salesforce object/field.
  3. Staged migration: parent objects (Accounts) first, then children (Contacts, Opportunities).
  4. Tool selection: Data Loader for bulk, Informatica/MuleSoft for complex transforms.
  5. Validation: pre-migration counts, post-migration reconciliation reports.
  6. Freeze/cutover plan to minimize downtime.
  7. Always have a rollback plan.

Q: A client says "Salesforce is too slow." What do you do?

Diagnose before guessing. Common culprits: unindexed custom fields in queries, list views hitting SOQL limits, Visualforce pages without server-side pagination, too many real-time callouts on page load, or missing caching. I use Debug Logs, Event Monitoring, and Salesforce Optimizer to pinpoint the actual cause, then fix the root issue — not the symptom.

Q: Stakeholders want a feature that violates Salesforce best practices. What do you do?

I explain the risk clearly: technical debt, future maintenance cost, governor limit exposure, upgrade risk. I propose an alternative that meets the business need within best practices. If they insist after understanding the risk, I document the decision and my recommendation in writing, then implement it as safely as possible.

Consultant interviews reward structure. Even a partial answer scored well when I walked through discovery → design → risk → recommendation instead of jumping straight to a technical fix.
