Expanding Support for Scientists Q&A — Anthropic's Research Push

August 2026  ·  9 questions

Anthropic announced a major expansion of its support for scientific research, centered on a new Claude team plan for scientists and a broadening of scope beyond biology into other compute-intensive fields. This Q&A covers the new plan, how it relates to Claude Science and the AI for Science program, who qualifies, and the safety guardrails still in place for sensitive research areas.

The New Claude Team Plan

1.What is the new Claude team plan for scientists?

A dedicated Claude team plan offering 10,000 free and discounted subscriptions to researchers globally for one year. Standard seats are complimentary, while premium seats with 5x usage limits cost $15 per month.

2.Who is eligible to register for the plan?

Principal investigators and equivalent leaders at academic or nonprofit institutions can register through a verification form. Once verified, team leaders can add lab members to their plan.

3.Can researchers get additional compute or usage beyond the team plan?

Yes — researchers can apply separately for up to $50,000 in additional credits per project through the existing AI for Science program, on top of whatever seat they hold on the new team plan.

How It Fits With Existing Programs

4.How does this relate to Claude Science?

Claude Science, launched in June 2026, is an integrated research workbench that pairs Claude with computing resources for scientific work. The new team plan builds on top of that existing foundation rather than replacing it.

5.What is the AI for Science program, and how is it different from the new team plan?

AI for Science is Anthropic's existing program providing complimentary credits to high-impact researchers on a project basis (up to $50,000 per project). The new team plan is broader and more accessible — a subscription-based offering aimed at getting Claude into the hands of many more labs, rather than a smaller number of high-impact, credit-funded projects.

6.What kind of research has Anthropic previously highlighted as coming out of these programs?

The announcement pointed to past contributions such as work on the Riemann zeta function and advances in protein design as examples of the caliber of research these programs have supported.

Scope and Safety

7.Is this expansion limited to biological sciences?

No — that's the core change. Anthropic's scientist support had previously focused on biological sciences, and this expansion broadens it to other fields, including compute-intensive research areas beyond biology.

8.Are there any restrictions for biology and chemistry researchers specifically?

Yes — researchers working in biology and chemistry remain restricted to Opus-class models as a safety measure, even as the broader program expands to other disciplines.

9.Is there a separate access path for life sciences professionals who need more capable models?

Yes — Anthropic partners with the U.S. government on an access program for life sciences professionals that permits use of Mythos-class models under that arrangement, with initial participants already enrolled.
